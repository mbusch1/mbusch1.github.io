I enjoy mentoring, and I think radio astronomy is an unusually good field to learn research in:
the data are public, the software is open, and a motivated student can go from raw telescope
output to a real measurement in a single summer.

My approach centers on helping students take intellectual ownership of their research at a pace
that fits their interests, strengths, and longer-term goals. I work with each student to define
milestones, build the technical skills the project needs, and develop confidence working
independently. I try hard to make room for the things that actually advance a career — going to
conferences, meeting people, joining collaborations — and to keep the environment inclusive and
grounded in curiosity about the underlying physics.

If you are a student interested in the interstellar medium, radio spectroscopy, or working with
data from the GBT, VLA, ALMA, or Murriyang, please email me at
[mpbusch@nrao.edu](mailto:mpbusch@nrao.edu). Tell me a bit about what you have done and what
you are curious about — a polished CV is not a prerequisite.

Teaching
======

I am certified by [The Carpentries](https://carpentries.org/) to teach data-intensive computing
through live-coding Python workshops. At UC San Diego I taught these for the **STARTastro**
program, which supports undergraduate transfer students in building research-ready computational
skills, along with a shorter scientific Python workshop in the Transfer Student Research Workshop
Series. Earlier, at Johns Hopkins, I was a teaching assistant for several undergraduate physics
courses, leading lab sections and recitations.
