A blog on an academic website is a promise most of us do not keep. You set it up with good
intentions, write two posts in the first month, and then a proposal deadline arrives and the
thing sits there, quietly advertising the date you last had free time.

So: **Radio Silence**. The name is a small joke at my own expense, and also an accurate
description of the posting schedule. It sets expectations honestly, and it happens to be a term
of art in my field.

What I expect to write about here, when I write:

- **Things I found interesting in the data.** Usually about molecular gas that is not where the
  CO maps say it should be.
- **Practical radio astronomy.** Reduction, calibration, and the specific ways spectral-line
  data will mislead you if you let it. The kind of thing that lives in people's heads and
  never makes it into a paper.
- **Notes from telescopes.** Green Bank, the VLA, Murriyang.
- **The job itself.** Fellowships, mentoring, and what the day-to-day of being a postdoc in
  radio astronomy actually looks like.

If you want the formal version of what I work on, the [research page](/research/) and the
[publications page](/publications/) are more reliable than this will be.
